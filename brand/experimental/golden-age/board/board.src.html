Card Hunt · brand exploration · golden age, 1993–98

The Card Hunt Bestiary

Three ways the monkey could belong to a forgotten card set. Each plate shows the silhouette, palette, mark, icon and a card. Plate II is finished pixel art. Plates I and III wait on painted illustrations, so their art windows hold a silhouette study.

What the references say

THE CARD SCANS

  • The art window is small, so each card carries one subject, one light source and a lot of dark around it.
  • Frames are printed textures, not ornament: beaded black, marbled blue, mottled white, brown parchment for artifacts.
  • Colour stays earthy and muted, with a single saturated accent: a flame, a jewel or a sky.
  • Mystery comes from what's hidden. Creatures are half-lit and seen at odd angles, never posed like a mascot.

THE APP TODAY

  • Dark-first, Shop-style layout. Real card images carry most of the colour on screen.
  • The header is plain "Card Hunt" at about 30 px, so the mark has to read at that size, and the icon at 16 to 48 px.
  • The purple accent fights every golden-age palette. In this theme, tarnished gold or brass should replace it.

WHAT THAT MEANS FOR THE MONKEY

  • A silhouette you can read in one shape: hat brim, tail curl and the lens are the three hooks.
  • Lean, real primate anatomy, smaller eyes and a knowing look. No plush proportions.
  • Type set separately from the art, so CARD HUNT stays crisp at every size.
PLATE I · CLASSIC PAINTED

The Archivist

A sly scholar crouched on old tomes in a candlelit vault, lens raised to one amber eye. This plate draws on the Alpha, Legends and The Dark mood: one candle, deep black, gothic quiet. The lens-to-eye pose is the most ownable silhouette of the three. It says "hunt" with no words and still reads at 16 px.

PROMPT FOR THE PAINTING
The Archivist. A small, clever monkey scholar with intelligent amber eyes, lean wiry anatomy and a weathered, stained pith helmet, crouched on a stack of ancient leather-bound tomes in a candlelit underground archive. He holds a tarnished brass magnifying glass up to one eye, and the lens enlarges and lights that eye. In his other hand is a single trading card with a faint golden glow. A single candle at his feet lights him from below. Behind him, vaulted stone shelves fade into deep black. Traditional hand-painted fantasy illustration in the style of early-1990s fantasy trading cards and paperback covers, oil and acrylic on board, visible brushwork, textured paint, dramatic chiaroscuro, palette of lamp-black, umber, oxblood, candle gold and vellum, subtle imperfections, mysterious atmosphere. No text, no border, not cartoonish, not a mascot.
PLATE II · PIXEL ART · FINISHED

The Rooftop Thief

Mid-leap over moonlit bazaar domes, card clutched to his chest, magnifier curled in his tail and a red scarf streaming behind. This plate draws on Arabian Nights and the 16-bit games of the same years. It's drawn pixel by pixel at 160×128 with 43 colours in short ramps, dithering only where two bands meet, and outlines tinted from each part's darkest shade. It carries the energy, the animation and the loading screens.

PLATE III · HYBRID

The Relic Seeker

An old explorer standing upright on a great dead gear, lifting a glowing card out of a sleeping brass automaton. This plate draws on Antiquities and early Urza's Saga: verdigris, clockwork and reverent light. Painted creature, pixel-drawn brass ornament and the brown artifact frame. It's the most "legendary" of the three and suits card backs and the loading screen.

PROMPT FOR THE PAINTING
The Relic Seeker. An old, wise monkey explorer with grey-streaked fur, lean believable anatomy, a battered pith helmet and a bronze-buckled leather harness, standing upright on a huge broken gear in a ruined temple full of dormant clockwork. He lifts a glowing trading card out of the open chest of a sleeping brass automaton, and its green-gold light catches his face. A tarnished magnifying glass hangs from a chain at his hip. Dusty rays fall from a crack in the ceiling. Traditional hand-painted fantasy illustration in the style of early-1990s fantasy trading cards and paperback covers, oil and acrylic on board, visible brushwork, dramatic chiaroscuro, palette of slate, verdigris, old brass, relic-glow green and rust, reverent and slightly eerie. MY RECOMMENDATION

Make the Archivist the mascot and the Thief his pixel double

The lens-to-the-eye pose is the strongest single idea. It's the only silhouette that says "hunting" by itself, and its icon still reads at 16 px. Plate II is already finished, and its energy belongs in the moving parts: loading screen, card flip and empty states. The Relic Seeker's verdigris world is the best fit for the card back. Paint the Archivist first. When that image comes in, I'll redraw the Thief sprite so both share one face and one hat.
